## The three options compared

- Shopify: fastest to launch, predictable monthly cost, excellent apps and payments, but you pay transaction fees and are limited by its rules

## Scaling is a journey, not a disaster

Many apps are built for the user count they have, then slow down or break as they grow. Scaling does not have to be painful if you plan for it at the right moments.

## What breaks first

- The database, which usually becomes the bottleneck before anything else
- The server, if everything runs on a single machine
- Slow queries that were fine with small data and are unusable with large data
- Background jobs piling up during busy periods
- Assets and media served from the same server as your app

## The practical fixes

- Optimise the slow database queries first, as this is usually the biggest win
- Cache answers that do not change often, so the database works less
- Move media and static files to a content delivery network
- Add more servers behind a load balancer when one machine is not enough
- Offload slow work to background processing instead of blocking requests

## The over-engineering trap

Building for millions of users when you have hundreds wastes budget and slows you down. Scale in response to real growth, not imagined growth.
