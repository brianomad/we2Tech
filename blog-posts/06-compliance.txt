## Privacy law applies to apps

If your app collects personal data from users in Hong Kong, it is covered by the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance, the PDPO. Many app owners discover this only after a complaint or a fine.

## What counts as personal data

Any data that identifies a person: names, phone numbers, email addresses, locations, device identifiers, payment details and even data that becomes identifying when combined. Analytics and marketing tracking collects personal data too.

## The duties that apply to your app

- Inform users clearly what data you collect and why, before you collect it
- Get consent where required, especially for direct marketing
- Only collect what is needed for your purpose, not everything possible
- Keep data secure and delete it when it is no longer needed
- Honour access and correction requests from users

Direct marketing has strict rules requiring consent and an opt-out. Overseas data transfers have their own safeguards. And the anti-doxxing amendments have sharpened the penalties around data misuse.

Privacy is far easier to build into an app from the start than to retrofit. A clear privacy policy, minimal collection and solid security are the foundations. ## Two privacy regimes, two questions

The GDPR is the European data protection law, and the PDPO is Hong Kong's. Their names get used interchangeably, but they are different laws with different requirements, and your obligations depend on whom you serve.

## The PDPO in short

Hong Kong's law applies to personal data collected in Hong Kong. It is built around data protection principles: fair collection, purpose limitation, security, retention limits and user access rights. Direct marketing has strict consent rules.

## The GDPR in short

The GDPR applies to anyone processing data of people in the European Economic Area, wherever the business sits. It is broader and more prescriptive, with a data protection by design requirement, mandatory breach notification and heavier potential fines.

## Where they overlap

Both require transparency about collection, security for stored data and rights for individuals to access and correct their data. Doing the basics well covers a large part of both laws.

## What Hong Kong businesses should do

If you only serve Hong Kong, the PDPO is your priority. If you serve European customers, GDPR applies to that data regardless of where you are. Many Hong Kong businesses with global customers end up aligning with the stricter standard.

Start with honest collection practices and clear policies, which serve you under either law. The we2Tech team can help you build a privacy foundation that works across markets.

## The password problem

Passwords get stolen, reused and guessed, no matter how careful users are. Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second check so that knowing a password is no longer enough to get in.

## How it works

The first factor is something you know, the password. The second is something you have or are: a code from an authenticator app, a confirmation prompt on a phone, a physical security key or a biometric like a fingerprint. Both must match before access is granted.

## Where it matters most

- Admin and staff accounts on your systems, where one breach compromises everything
- Accounts with access to money, customer data or sensitive business information
- Developer accounts, because code compromises can spread widely
- Email accounts, which are the keys to resetting everything else

## The honest limits

2FA blocks the most common attacks, but it is not a cure-all. Phishing can still fool users, and some methods are stronger than others. It is a vital layer, not the whole wall.
